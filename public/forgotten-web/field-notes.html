Field Notes

These are not scientific findings. I have no sample size, no control group, and no funding. What follows is just what I have noticed, over years of losing umbrellas and watching other people lose theirs, written down because somebody should.

On the moment of loss

Nobody ever notices the exact moment an umbrella is lost. It is always discovered afterward, usually by absence rather than by witness: you reach for it and it is not there. This is different from losing a phone or a wallet, which tend to announce their own disappearance quickly, through a pat of the pocket or a dead screen. An umbrella's absence is quiet. It waits for rain to reveal itself.

On where they end up

In my own experience, lost umbrellas accumulate in a small number of predictable places: under cafe tables, propped by the door of a building you were only visiting once, in the overhead racks of trains, and inside lost-property boxes that nobody with the missing umbrella ever thinks to check. I believe this is less about carelessness and more about the shape of the object itself --- long, thin, and easy to set down somewhere that is not your hand.

On why cheap umbrellas fail first

It is my opinion, formed from personal experience rather than any structural testing, that the cheapest umbrellas are also the ones most likely to turn inside out in a strong gust, and that this is not entirely a coincidence: a cheap umbrella is bought in a hurry, by someone unprepared for weather, and used carelessly because it was never expected to last. The umbrella inherits the mood of its purchase.

On the guilt

Losing an umbrella produces a strange, disproportionate guilt --- out of scale with the actual cost of replacing it. I think this is because an umbrella is one of the few objects we buy specifically to protect ourselves, and losing it feels like a small failure of self-care, not just a missing item. That, more than anything, is why this shrine exists: the loss deserves somewhere to go besides a shrug.
