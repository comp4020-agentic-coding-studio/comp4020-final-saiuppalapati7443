The Collection

These are my own umbrellas, the ones I personally lost, in roughly the order I lost them. Each one was a different umbrella, bought for a different reason, and each one disappeared in a different way. I think that variety matters. An umbrella is not interchangeable with another umbrella just because they are both, technically, umbrellas.

Big Blue

The golf umbrella my father gave me for my first year of university. Enormous, navy, built like it expected to survive a cyclone. I left it propped against a lecture theatre door and walked home in the rain rather than go back for it, which tells you something about how little I understood its value at the time. I understand it now.

The Clear One

A transparent dome umbrella, the kind you see in old photos of London. I liked it because you could see the rain hitting it from underneath, which felt like a small privilege. Left on a train somewhere between two stations whose names I no longer remember, which feels appropriate for an umbrella defined by being easy to see through and easy to look past.

The Cheap One From the Servo

Bought in a genuine emergency, mid-downpour, from a servo that charged roughly four times what the umbrella was worth and was entirely justified in doing so. It lasted three weeks and two storms before a gust turned it inside out beyond repair. I do not resent it. It did exactly the job it was bought to do, and no umbrella should be judged by a standard it was never built to meet.

Grandma's Umbrella

A heavy wooden-handled umbrella that belonged to my grandmother before it belonged to me. This is the loss I think about the most, because it was not really mine to lose. It went missing from a cafe on a day I was in a hurry, and I did not notice until I was already three suburbs away. If anyone reading this has ever found a wooden-handled umbrella with a small brass ferrule and wondered whose it was: it was hers, and then it was mine, and now it is wherever it is.
